# Interface copy standard

Kall copy should tell the user what record, decision, evidence, or action is on
screen. Broad motivational phrases and internal product terms do not explain the
work well enough.

## Voice

- Name the object: role, resume, achievement, application, criterion, or career
  direction.
- State what the user can decide or change on this screen.
- Explain what Kall uses and what it will do before asking for an action.
- Keep consequential actions reviewable in the copy. Kall may prepare, compare,
  or propose; the user approves.
- Prefer plain labels such as `Evidence`, `Tracked roles`, and `Company boards`
  over internal labels such as `Intelligence`, `Discovery`, or `Sources`.
- Avoid broad phrases that could belong to any career product, including
  `make your next move`, `what comes next`, and `your experience, ready`.

## Page structure

The eyebrow names the area. The heading names the decision or job to be done.
The description explains the inputs, result, or boundary. Buttons use the action
and object together, such as `Compare role`, `Review application`, or `Search
open roles`.

Loading, empty, error, and disabled states remain factual. They say what is being
loaded, what is missing, what was preserved after failure, and what action can
recover. Scores and recommendations say whether they use stored facts,
documented rules, or AI suggestions.
